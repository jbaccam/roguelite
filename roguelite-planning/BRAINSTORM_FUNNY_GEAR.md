# Brainstorm — Funny Weapons and Retired Armor Ideas

> **Current decision:** The game has no separate armor equipment or armor-set system, and equipped weapons remain visible as floating models around the avatar. Armor concepts below are retained only as an idea archive for possible passive-item names, lobby props, or non-core customization—not as the current equipment plan.

**Status:** Idea pool, not an approved catalog  
**Direction:** Roblox avatars accumulate ridiculous but readable attack systems in the HUD and around them during attacks. The arsenal deliberately mixes real weapons and nonsense; the contrast is more important than making every object a joke.

## Confirmed presentation rules

- Melee weapons auto-attack without requiring the avatar's limbs or weapon model to make physical contact.
- Melee timing is communicated through slash arcs, thrust streaks, spin rings, shockwaves, and impact effects.
- The avatar holds no weapon. Equipped models remain visible in a floating formation, independently aim and perform their own attacks, then recover. Effects reinforce the model motion. See FLOATING_WEAPON_PRESENTATION.md; this supersedes the old effect-only direction.
- Ranged weapons produce visible projectiles.
- Projectile size, speed, color, and trail should make the weapon recognizable during a crowded run.

## Design rule

A funny name is not enough. Each item needs:

1. an immediately recognizable 3D silhouette;
2. a combat behavior the player can understand without reading;
3. an upgrade path with a satisfying visual escalation;
4. a reason to fit at least one build;
5. effects that do not hide mobs or hazards.

The joke should survive longer than a current meme. Everyday objects, bad safety equipment, food, office supplies, sports gear, and household junk will age better than references to one viral trend.

## Class weapon distribution

**Confirmed structure:** Weapon counts remain equal across classes. The player selects a class before the chapter, and the guaranteed first weapon must belong to that class. A new class begins with its signature weapon; after unlocking other weapons in that class, the player may select any owned class weapon as the starter.

Production targets stay even at every milestone:

- prototype: six classes × one signature weapon = **6 weapons**;
- vertical slice: six classes × two weapons = **12 weapons**;
- launch target: six classes × six weapons = **36 weapons**.

Proposed 36-weapon allocation:

| Class | Signature starter | Other class weapons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brawler** | Frying Pan | Nunchucks, Katana, Kusarigama, Spatula, Baseball Bat |
| **Gunner** | Glock | Draco, Fart Gun, Shotgun, T-Shirt Cannon, Rocket Launcher |
| **Thrower** | Boomerang | Kunais, Molotovs, Eggs, Steak, Deck of Cards |
| **Juggler** | Boxing Gloves | Rubber Ducks, Cinder Blocks, Yo-Yo, Bowling Ball, Bowling Pins |
| **Handyman** | Nail Gun | Wrecking Ball, Shovel, Paint Roller, Vacuum Cleaner, Power Washer |
| **Mage** | Magic Staff | Mjölnir, Excalibur, Pandora's Box, Medusa's Head, Crystal Ball |

Class assignment controls starter eligibility, affinity milestones, and one shop-weighting pool—not a hard ban on hybrid builds. Every unlocked off-class weapon remains eligible. Two and four equipped home-class weapons activate class bonuses, deliberately leaving the fifth slot free for experimentation. Cross-class combat tags also matter: Nail Gun belongs to Handyman but its `Gun`, `Rapid`, and `Projectile` tags allow Gunner bonuses and related shop weighting to affect it.

## Combination-tier rule

Combining identical weapons must change how they behave, not merely increase damage. Each weapon has a four-step temporary transformation:

1. **Run Tier I — Base:** clear core behavior.
2. **Run Tier II — Practical upgrade:** improves consistency, coverage, magazine, return path, orbit count, or another visible limitation.
3. **Run Tier III — Signature modification:** adds the weapon's defining mechanic.
4. **Run Tier IV — Payoff:** creates a strong, readable final form without filling the screen.

Example Glock chain:

| Combination | Result | Mechanical change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 × Glock | **Glock + Extended Clip** | Larger magazine, longer firing sequence, less time reloading |
| 2 × Glock + Extended Clip | **Glock with Switch** | Fires automatically in rapid bursts with controlled spread |
| 2 × Glock with Switch | **Akimbo Switches** | Fires two alternating streams toward nearby priority targets |

Damage and cooldown can also improve at every tier, but those numerical buffs support the visible mechanical change rather than replace it. Every weapon needs its complete Tier I–IV chain designed before it enters the production roster.

## Restrained mythological weapons

Mythology should supply rare spectacle, not define every chapter or turn the game into a medieval fantasy clone.

| Weapon | Availability | Attack behavior | Upgrade payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mjölnir | Confirmed later weapon | The hammer flashes to a target, creates a lightning impact, and visibly returns | Impacts chain lightning; the final return causes thunder around the avatar |
| Excalibur | Later candidate | Golden slash effects target large groups without requiring a physical swing | Oversized sword-light beams descend on marked groups |
| Pandora's Box | Later candidate | Opens periodically and chooses one of several clearly telegraphed effects | Gains better outcomes as Luck rises, but retains controlled unpredictability |
| Medusa's Head | Later candidate | Projects a short gaze cone that slows and eventually petrifies ordinary mobs | Petrified mobs shatter into damaging fragments |
| Midas Touch | Later candidate | Golden hand impacts mark a limited number of mobs | Marked mobs can drop temporary run currency without enabling infinite farming |

Do not add a large pantheon checklist. Two or three memorable myth weapons are stronger than every famous god and artifact competing for the same fantasy space.

## Magic direction

Start with one **Magic Staff** rather than separate generic Fire, Ice, Lightning, Poison, and Dark Staves. Its build combination determines the spell package:

| Pairing direction | Staff evolution | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area/burning passive | Meteor Staff | Slow visible bolts create compact burning impacts |
| Slow/recovery passive | Snow-Day Staff | Bolts split into ice shards and slow groups |
| Chain/attack-speed passive | Power-Outage Staff | Bolts jump between targets with readable lightning links |
| Projectile-size passive | Bubble Staff | Large slow bubbles trap light mobs and pop |
| Luck/random passive | Bad Magician's Staff | Alternates between several controlled spell effects |

This lets magic exist without filling the roster with interchangeable colored sticks.

## Armor philosophy

Armor should be visually modular enough to fit many Roblox avatars without destroying their identity. Oversized helmets, shoulder pieces, backpacks, waist items, and floating accessories are safer than replacing the entire avatar body.

Two possible systems remain open:

- **Persistent sets:** Earn pieces outside runs and select a kit before entering. This risks resembling Final Swarm and makes balance/monetization more sensitive.
- **Run armor:** Find or choose armor during the eight-minute chapter; it disappears afterward like other build pieces. This makes the avatar visibly transform during each run.
- **Hybrid:** Persistent armor is cosmetic, while run-found “gadgets” provide temporary stats and effects. This is the cleanest separation between identity, progression, and fair monetization.

## Avoiding similarity to Final Swarm

- Use improvised everyday comedy instead of medieval/fantasy gear.
- Make the player's changing build visually ridiculous rather than a conventional rarity ladder of stronger swords and armor.
- Let map geometry and local mob factions define chapters.
- Avoid copying its chest probabilities, armor-count structure, weapon roster, rarity tiers, or persistent power economy.
- Prefer gameplay unlocks and deterministic progress over paid randomized power chests.
